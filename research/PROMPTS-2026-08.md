## Prompt 1 — Regulatory research

> You are a regulatory research analyst. Today is 2026-08-26. Two students in Lithuania (EU) want to start a business that (a) publishes crypto trading signals (spot and possibly perpetual futures on centralised exchanges) to subscribers via Telegram/Discord for a monthly fee, and/or (b) earns exchange referral/affiliate commissions by sending their audience to crypto exchanges. Signals would be NON-personalised (same broadcast to everyone), signal-only (no order execution, no managing client funds).
>
> Use WebSearch and WebFetch to establish, with primary or authoritative sources (EUR-Lex, ESMA, Bank of Lithuania / lb.lt, reputable law-firm briefings), the CURRENT position on each of the following. Do not rely on memory for anything that could have changed since 2025 — verify it.
>
> 1. MiCA (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114): Does providing non-personalised crypto trading signals to paying subscribers constitute "providing advice on crypto-assets" (Art. 3(1)(24)) or any other crypto-asset service requiring CASP authorisation? Quote the definition. Note any ESMA Q&A or guidance on signal groups, "finfluencers", or general recommendations.
> 2. Crypto derivatives (perpetual futures, CFDs on crypto): are these MiFID II financial instruments per ESMA, and if a signal channel repeatedly recommends trades in them, do the Market Abuse Regulation (MAR, Reg. 596/2014) Art. 20 investment-recommendation rules and Delegated Regulation (EU) 2016/958 apply to non-professional "experts"? What disclosures are then required (identity, date/time, conflicts, past-recommendation record)? Note ESMA's 2024–2026 statements on social-media investment recommendations/finfluencers if any.
> 3. Lithuania specifically: the Bank of Lithuania's position on crypto signal providers / advice; MiCA transitional period end date in Lithuania; any warnings issued about signal groups; what a small Lithuanian company (MB) publishing signals would need to comply with (consumer protection, advertising rules for financial/crypto products).
> 4. Affiliate/referral marketing: Under MiCA, may an EU-based affiliate promote exchanges to EU residents? Which major exchanges hold MiCA CASP authorisation as of mid-2026 (check Binance, Bybit, OKX, Kraken, Coinbase, Bitget, KuCoin, Crypto.com, Bitpanda, Bitvavo, MEXC)? Any ESMA/NCAs warnings about affiliates promoting non-authorised exchanges, and the reverse-solicitation guidelines?
> 5. Tax/entity basics for Lithuania in 2026: is a "mažoji bendrija" (MB) or "individuali veikla" the common vehicle for two founders; current corporate income tax rate for small companies; VAT registration threshold. (Brief; cite lb.lt / vmi.lt / official sources.)
>
> Return a concise, structured report (max ~900 words) with: a per-topic finding, confidence level (high/medium/low), what is still uncertain, and a Sources list of the exact URLs you used. Flag clearly anything where the rules are ambiguous or where a lawyer's opinion would be needed before launch. Do not invent citations — only list URLs you actually fetched or that appeared in search results with matching content.

## Prompt 2 — Market research

> You are a commercially minded market analyst. Today is 2026-08-26. Two students in Lithuania with 3+ years of crypto/trading experience each, €1,000–5,000 of total risk capital, 10–20 hours/week each, and NO existing audience want to build a semi-passive income business. They are considering (A) a paid crypto trading-signal community and/or (B) exchange referral income and/or (C) "reselling"-based income (retail arbitrage, e-commerce reselling, reselling software/services).
>
> Use WebSearch/WebFetch to gather CURRENT (2025–2026) evidence for each of the following. Prefer primary sources (platform pricing pages, official affiliate program pages, credible surveys). Verify everything — do not rely on memory.
>
> 1. Paid signal/trading communities: typical monthly price points (Telegram/Discord/Whop trading communities), typical conversion from free to paid, churn benchmarks for trading communities, and what top-performing communities do to build trust (verified track records, public timestamped calls, third-party verification tools such as Myfxbook-style services for crypto, e.g. any that track Telegram signal channels). Which platforms do paid trading communities use to charge (Whop, Telegram Stars/paid subscriptions, Discord paid memberships, Patreon, Stripe) and what fees do those platforms charge?
> 2. Exchange referral/affiliate programs in 2026: commission structures (percentage of referred users' trading fees, duration, caps) for Bybit, Binance, OKX, Bitget, Kraken, MEXC, and any EU-regulated exchanges (Bitvavo, Bitpanda, Kraken EU). Note any restrictions for EU residents/affiliates or any program changes in 2025–2026. What is a realistic monthly revenue per active referred trader (evidence, not guesses)?
> 3. Reselling models with €1–5k capital from Lithuania/EU: retail arbitrage (Amazon.de FBA, eBay, Vinted), dropshipping, print-on-demand, software/hosting reselling. For each, cite typical net margins, time requirement, capital tie-up, and failure rates or common pitfalls. Be sober: which of these is actually "passive"?
> 4. Realistic income timelines: any credible data on how long it takes new creators/communities to reach €500/month and €2,000/month (creator economy surveys, Whop/Gumroad/Patreon published stats).
> 5. Costs of running a signal business: Telegram bot hosting, market-data APIs (free vs paid tiers), payment processing, a basic landing page — a rough monthly cost stack for a lean setup.
>
> Return a concise, structured report (max ~900 words): per-topic findings with numbers, confidence levels, and a Sources list of the exact URLs used. Distinguish clearly between verified figures and estimates. Do not invent numbers or citations.
