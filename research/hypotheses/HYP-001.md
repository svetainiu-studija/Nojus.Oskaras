# HYP-001

Drafted 2026-08-27 from the founders' interview answers (style: swing 2–10 days; dips in uptrends were the money-maker over 3 years; BTC filter = above 50-day MA; indicators used: MAs 20/50/200, RSI, volume). Founders: correct anything that is not how you actually trade. Parameters marked **fixed** are set a priori and must not be optimised — only reported for sensitivity.

```
ID: HYP-001
Name: Pullback-to-EMA20 in a confirmed uptrend, BTC-filtered (swing)
Setup (what you look for, in rules a computer can check):
  Daily bars. All of the following on the coin:
  - Uptrend: close > EMA50 > EMA200 (fixed), and a new 20-day high occurred
    within the last 15 bars (recent strength, not a stale trend).
  - Pullback: at least 2 consecutive down-closes, and the low of the current
    bar is within 2% of EMA20 or below it, while close stays above EMA50 (fixed).
  - Pullback is orderly: pullback-bar volumes each < 20-day average volume,
    and RSI(14) between 35 and 55 (not oversold — oversold is HYP-003 territory).
  - BTC filter: BTC close > its 50-day SMA (fixed).
  - Listing age >= 90 days; pair in the point-in-time top-30 liquidity universe.
Entry rule:
  Trigger = first daily close above the previous bar's high after the pullback
  bar, with RSI(14) higher than the previous bar (turning up).
  Buy at the next daily open (plus modelled delay/slippage).
  Skip the trade if the stop distance (entry -> invalidation) exceeds 8%.
Exit rules (target / invalidation / time stop):
  Invalidation: close below the lowest low of the pullback (min low of the
  last 3 bars before the trigger). Hard stop at that level, executed at the
  next open after a close below it (spot: no intrabar guarantees).
  Target: take 50% at 2R; trail the remainder at the 10-day low.
  Time stop: exit at market after 10 bars if neither hit.
  Regime exit: flatten if BTC closes below its 50-day SMA.
Position sizing rule:
  Risk 1% of equity per trade (distance entry -> invalidation defines size);
  6-position / 6%-heat caps (D-015). Max 2 positions in coins whose 30-day
  correlation to each other > 0.8 (avoid buying the same dip five times).
Universe (pairs, timeframe):
  Point-in-time top-30 OKX spot pairs by trailing 90-day quote volume, daily
  bars; USDT quote. Listing age >= 90 days. (Survivorship note: the current
  dataset is a present-day snapshot — see the WP1 review; run the always-in-
  universe subset as a mandatory sensitivity.)
Why it should work (who is on the other side and why they lose):
  In a trend, pullbacks are driven by short-term profit taking and by
  leveraged longs being flushed (funding resets); that selling is time-limited.
  Slower, larger trend buyers re-enter near the moving average. The losers are
  (a) late shorts who fade the pullback into support and cover on the reclaim,
  and (b) weak-hand longs who sold the low and buy back higher. Our edge, if
  real, comes from selectivity — trend + BTC filter + orderly pullback + a
  reclaim trigger — not from speed. The two filters that separate us from
  "buy every dip" are the BTC regime and the non-oversold RSI band.
What would make you abandon it:
  Walk-forward net expectancy <= 0; or the BTC + trend filters do not improve
  results over unfiltered EMA20 dips (then it is beta, not edge); or > 70% of
  profit comes from <= 3 trades or from a single year (2021 or 2024); or the
  2x cost stress drops PF below 1.1; or it fails the random-entry baseline.
Your discretionary experience with it (years, rough hit rate, worst period):
  ~3 years; this is the style that actually made money for both founders.
  Hit rate never measured (founders' estimate: ___ %).
  Worst period: 2025 — founders to add what went wrong (which months, and
  whether it was dips in a downtrend, stops too tight, or size).
Data not available in v1 (deliberately excluded, revisit later):
  Order-book/liquidity depth, funding, open interest, on-chain — the dataset
  is OHLCV only. Funding-rate history from OKX is a candidate v2 filter.
Priority: TEST FIRST (Sprint A).
```
