ID: HYP-002
Name: Relative-strength leaders bought on a volume-confirmed 20-day-high breakout
Setup (what you look for, in rules a computer can check):
  Daily bars.
  - Leader: coin's 60-day return minus BTC's 60-day return ranks in the top 5
    of the point-in-time universe (fixed: 60 days, top 5).
  - Breakout: today's close is a new 20-day high (fixed) and the bar's volume
    >= 1.5x the 20-day average volume (fixed).
  - Not extended: close is less than 15% above EMA20 (skip parabolic bars —
    chasers' territory).
  - BTC filter: BTC close > its 50-day SMA (fixed).
  - Listing age >= 90 days.
Entry rule:
  Buy at the next daily open after the breakout close (plus modelled delay
  and slippage). Skip if the stop distance exceeds 10%.
Exit rules (target / invalidation / time stop):
  Invalidation: close below the 10-day low at entry (hard stop, executed at
  the next open after a close below it).
  Trail: once the trade is +1R, trail the stop at the 10-day low.
  Target: none fixed — momentum edge lives in the tail; take 1/3 at 3R only
  to stabilise the equity curve.
  Relative-strength exit: exit at market if the coin drops out of the top
  half of the relative-strength ranking.
  Time stop: 15 bars if not +1R by then.
  Regime exit: flatten if BTC closes below its 50-day SMA.
Position sizing rule:
  Risk 1% of equity per trade; 6-position / 6%-heat caps (D-015).
  Max 2 concurrent leaders from the same sector/narrative if a sector map
  exists; otherwise the correlation cap from HYP-001.
Universe (pairs, timeframe):
  Point-in-time top-30 OKX spot pairs by trailing 90-day quote volume, daily
  bars. Same survivorship caveat and always-in-universe sensitivity as HYP-001.
Why it should work (who is on the other side and why they lose):
  Cross-sectional momentum is one of the better-documented effects in crypto
  (the same relative-strength persistence seen in equities). Mechanism: capital
  rotates slowly into the coins already attracting attention; holders of
  laggards rotate late and pay up; shorts positioned against "overextended"
  leaders are squeezed on the breakout; investors anchored to "it has already
  gone up too much" under-react. The volume filter separates institutional /
  sustained buying from thin-liquidity spikes; the "not extended" filter keeps
  us out of the trades where we ARE the chaser.
What would make you abandon it:
  Walk-forward net expectancy <= 0; or no improvement over equal-weight
  universe exposure during BTC-uptrend regimes (then it is beta); or turnover
  costs at 2x stress push PF below 1.1; or profits concentrated in 2021
  (mania) with nothing since; or the volume filter shows no separation from
  unfiltered breakouts; or it fails the random-entry baseline.
Your discretionary experience with it (years, rough hit rate, worst period):
  Founders trade breakouts and strong coins but have not tracked results by
