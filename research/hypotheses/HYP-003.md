# HYP-003

Drafted 2026-08-27 from the founders' interview answers. Reversals at extremes LOST money for the founders over 3 years. This hypothesis is the disciplined version of that style — reversals only inside an intact uptrend, only after a confirmed reclaim — so that the backtest can answer a specific question: did reversals lose because the style is bad, or because they were taken in downtrends without confirmation? If this fails too, reversals are dropped for good. Parameters marked **fixed** are set a priori and must not be optimised.

```
ID: HYP-003
Name: Capitulation reclaim inside an intact uptrend (reversal, with-trend only)
Setup (what you look for, in rules a computer can check):
  Daily bars. All of the following on the coin:
  - Higher-timeframe trend intact: close > EMA200 (fixed) — the drop is a
    flush inside a trend, not a trend change.
  - Capitulation: a drop of >= 15% from the 10-day high within <= 5 bars,
    making a 30-day low, with the capitulation bar's volume >= 2x the 20-day
    average (fixed) and RSI(14) < 30 on that bar (fixed).
  - BTC filter: BTC close > its 50-day SMA (fixed) — we are buying a coin-
    specific flush, not a market-wide crash.
  - Listing age >= 90 days.
Entry rule:
  Trigger = first daily close that is (a) above the previous bar's high and
  (b) above the midpoint of the capitulation bar (a real reclaim, not a bounce
  inside the wick). Buy at the next daily open. The trigger must occur within
  5 bars of the capitulation bar, otherwise the setup expires.
  Skip if the stop distance exceeds 10%.
Exit rules (target / invalidation / time stop):
  Invalidation: close below the capitulation low (hard stop at the next open
  after such a close).
  Target: take 50% at 2R or at EMA20, whichever comes first; trail the rest
  at the 5-day low.
  Time stop: 7 bars — mean reversion that has not happened in a week is not
  happening.
  Regime exit: flatten if BTC closes below its 50-day SMA.
Position sizing rule:
  Risk 1% of equity per trade; 6-position / 6%-heat caps (D-015).
  Additional cap: max 2 concurrent HYP-003 positions — flushes cluster.
Universe (pairs, timeframe):
  Point-in-time top-30 OKX spot pairs by trailing 90-day quote volume, daily
  bars. Same survivorship caveat and always-in-universe sensitivity.
Why it should work (who is on the other side and why they lose):
  Stop cascades and forced liquidations push price through fair value; the
  selling is mechanical and finite. Once forced sellers are exhausted, the
  imbalance reverts. The losers are the liquidated leveraged longs and the
  panic sellers who sold the low. The two conditions that separate this from
  "catching a falling knife" — the founders' historical mistake — are the
  intact higher-timeframe trend (more layers of forced selling remain in a
  downtrend) and waiting for the reclaim instead of buying the drop.
What would make you abandon it:
  Walk-forward net expectancy <= 0; or the reclaim confirmation shows no
  improvement over naive "buy the -15% day"; or it underperforms HYP-001 on
  the same capital with higher drawdown; or > 70% of profit comes from <= 3
  events; or it fails the random-entry baseline.
Your discretionary experience with it (years, rough hit rate, worst period):
  ~3 years of taking reversals discretionarily; net result: LOST money.
  Hit rate not measured. Worst period: 2025 — founders to add whether these
  were downtrend knife-catches (which this hypothesis excludes by rule).
Data not available in v1:
  Funding and OI (which would identify liquidation cascades directly) are not
  in the OHLCV dataset; strong v2 candidate if HYP-003 shows any signal.
Priority: THIRD (Sprint C, or drop if Sprints A/B need the time).
```
